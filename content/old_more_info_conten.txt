About ScanWait Wales
ScanWait Wales was produced as an accessibility tool by a group of outstandingly smart, attractive, philanthropic, humble students studying for a Data Science and Analytics MSc at Cardiff University.

ScanWait Wales is designed to help patients explore scanning wait times from NHS Wales and identify hospitals that may offer a selected scan. We are not affiliated with NHS Wales and we do not have access to NHS patient records, NHS numbers, clinical notes or individual appointment information. The estimated results do not represent an individual appointment. The application uses publicly available hospital information, postcode reference datasets and aggregated waiting-time statistics. No information is stored when using this service.

Our goal is to make publicly available information easier to understand and navigate. It is an information tool and does not provide individual appointment advice or determine clinical priority.

Data Privacy
The application only asks for the information necessary to perform the search: selected scan and, for the nearest hospital function, postcode.

The postcode is used temporarily to obtain coordinates and is never stored by the application's database. The application does not store combinations of postcode, scan selection, IP address or other identifiers. This is in accordance with the UK Data Protection Legislation Principles

Use of External Services
If you select to use the postcode distance tool, your postcode will then be sent to Postcodes.io. The services obtains approximate coordinates and sends them to the OpenStreetMap Routing Machine (OSRM) to calculate driving or walking information. We do not send any other data to external providers. We have used the UKSA data ethics checklists in the use of geospatial data to produce our postcode mapping feature.

Fairness and Inclusion
Ranking by straight-line distance does not always identify the hospital that is easiest to reach. Rural roads, public transport, mobility requirements and whether a user has access to a car may affect the real journey. Providing a nearest-three option with travel routing and an all-hospitals option helps users define what they think are their 'nearest' hospitals.

Accessability
The ScanWait Wales website has been designed and developed with the needs of individuals with visual and colour impairments or difficulty reading in mind.

Visual and Colour Impairment
The website's main font is Verdana - which was selected for its clarity. No text is smaller than 12pt, and the majority of it is a dark colour on a light (but not white) background to help with readability. The main site colours of white, red, and green were inspired by the Welsh flag. These colours have been adjusted in accordance with WCAG guidelines to provide sufficient contrast between text and the background. The primary background of the site is an off-white, which was selected to improve readability and reduce glare.
Colours outside the main scheme (used in visualisations) were chosen as they are visually distinct from the main theme, but still maintain a minimum contrast to comply with WCAG AA standards.
In addition to the above, we have chosen to avoid conveying important information exclusively through the use of colour. Hover text and varied opacity have been used in the visualisations where this is not possible.

Screen Readers
For visitors using screen readers, the number of links per page have been kept to a minimum and are sufficiently descriptive without requiring the additional context of the surrounding text. We have also chosen to avoid using emojis throughout the website. These decisions have been made to comply with the Equality Act 2010.
Colour contrasts were checked using the WebAIM contrast checker. Please note, our website has not been tested by blind or partially sighted users.

Language Options
Our application is available in both Welsh and English in line with the Welsh Language Measure 2011. We have provided Welsh and English versions that contain equivalent information.

Please note, there may be fewer errors in the Welsh. Cysill Ar-Lein was used to proof check the spelling and grammar but some errors may still have been missed. We are happy to take any feedback about improvements from users.

Methodology
What Data We Use
The official StatsWales dataset for all of our waiting-time records. We filter this data so that we only look at adult patients (18 years of age and over). Whenever you check a wait time, we calculate it using the most recent month

How We Work Out the Typical Wait
To find the typical (median) wait for a specific scan at your chosen hospital, we do the following:

- We add up all the patients currently waiting for that specific scan to get the total list size.
- We find the exact halfway point (50%) of that patient queue
- We count up through the weekly waiting bands until we pass that halfway point.
- To give you a safe and conservative estimate, we report the upper end of that weekly band.
- If the halfway point lands in the highest band (40 weeks and over), we simply report it as 40 weeks.

This method is referred to as a Cumulative Frequency Distribution. It is a simple and robust way to estimate wait times and is commonly used within healthcare. Its limitations lie in the fact that it is based on current trends rather than future predictions.

What the Wait Time Gauge Means
To make the wait times easy to read at a glance, we classify them into colour zones and use a visual speedometer: According to the Welsh government you should not be waiting more than 8 weeks for your scan.

- Green: A wait of 8 weeks or less. [within government targets]
- Orange: A wait between 9 and 12 weeks.
- Red: A wait of 13 weeks or longer.

Validation
New data is released towards the end of each month. Before we use that data, we inspect the data for any changes in its presentation and we calculate all possible outputs using a much slower but auditable method. These numbers are then checked against the output of our main calculator. We only use this new data when it passes all our checks.

Data Limitations
The data published from StatsWales is aggregated from monthly reports from all the health boards in Wales which is then used to measure their performance.

Urgency
It does not categorize data on 'urgency' therefore some patients who might have been classified as urgent would expect a shorter wait time. Consequently, a large influx of 'urgent' patients in a single month might mean that 'routine' patients could expect longer waits.

Incomplete Information on Scanning Locations
As of 2026, there is no publicly available database that contains information on what specific diagnostic services are offered in each and every hospital in Wales. Some hospitals mention it in their websites but the list is not comprehensive and most don't have that information readily available. We are working to request that information to be made public, as we believe that the public should know which services are available in their community.

Ghost Scanners
The data published by StatsWales contains some hospital waiting lists for scans which are not offered in that hospital. For example, a rural community hospital offering MRI scans. We try our best to filter out these obvious mistakes, but without a comprehensive hospital-scanner database, we can never be certain.
